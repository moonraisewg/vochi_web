HSK 5 is where most self-learners stall. HSK 1–4 vocabulary is concrete — food, weather, directions, things you can point at. HSK 5 adds 1,300 new words on top of HSK 4's 1,200, and a large share are abstract: theory, phenomenon, principle, viewpoint, competition. You can picture 苹果 (an apple). You cannot picture 观念 (a notion). That's the real difficulty spike, and why learners who breezed through HSK 3 and 4 hit a wall here. Below: what changes, the vocabulary grouped by theme, and how to drill it without burning out.

## What HSK 5 covers

- Abstract concepts and opinions (观念, 理论, 观点)
- Academic and research language (论文, 研究, 讲座)
- Workplace and professional life (员工, 部门, 业务)
- Media, public discourse and debate (媒体, 辩论, 反对)
- Emotional and psychological states (乐观, 悲观, 勇气)
- Society, economy and gradual change (竞争, 逐渐, 尽管)

**A note on the numbers.** China's Center for Language Education and Cooperation (the body long known as Hanban) finalized a restructured "HSK 3.0" syllabus in late 2025 that expands the test from 6 levels to 9 and roughly doubles vocabulary at every band — under it, Level 5 would require 3,600 cumulative words (1,600 new). As of this writing, though, the exam actually administered at most test centers is still the older HSK 2.0 standard, where Level 5 requires 2,500 cumulative words — 1,200 carried over from HSK 4 plus 1,300 new ones. This post uses the HSK 2.0 numbers, since that's what you'll be tested on unless your registration says otherwise. Confirm with your test center before you plan your studying around either figure.

If you haven't finished HSK 3 yet, start with the [HSK 3 vocabulary list](/tips/hsk-3-vocabulary-list-with-examples) first — this post assumes those 600 cumulative words are already solid. And if you've just worked through the connectors and opinion phrases in the [HSK 4 writing cheatsheet](/tips/hsk-4-writing-vocabulary-cheatsheet), HSK 5 is the natural next step: same skill (arguing a point), bigger vocabulary to do it with.

## Sample from each cluster

### Abstract concepts and opinions

- 观念 (guānniàn, concept/notion): 每个人的婚姻观念都不一样。*Everyone has a different notion of marriage.*
- 理论 (lǐlùn, theory): 这个理论听起来很有道理,但很难证明。*This theory sounds reasonable, but is hard to prove.*
- 观点 (guāndiǎn, viewpoint): 我同意你的观点,但角度不太一样。*I agree with your viewpoint, but from a different angle.*
- 原则 (yuánzé, principle): 诚实是我做人的原则。*Honesty is my principle in life.*
- 现象 (xiànxiàng, phenomenon): 这是一个很普遍的社会现象。*This is a very common social phenomenon.*

### Academic and research language

- 学术 (xuéshù, academic): 他对学术研究很感兴趣。*He's very interested in academic research.*
- 研究 (yánjiū, to research/research): 她正在研究一个新的课题。*She is researching a new topic.*
- 论文 (lùnwén, paper/thesis): 我的论文下个月就要交了。*My thesis is due next month.*
- 实验 (shíyàn, experiment): 这个实验做了三次才成功。*This experiment only succeeded on the third try.*
- 讲座 (jiǎngzuò, lecture): 今天下午有一个关于气候变化的讲座。*There's a lecture on climate change this afternoon.*

### Workplace and professional life

- 员工 (yuángōng, employee/staff): 公司一共有两百名员工。*The company has 200 employees in total.*
- 部门 (bùmén, department): 他刚被调到销售部门。*He was just transferred to the sales department.*
- 业务 (yèwù, business/operations): 这家公司的业务范围很广。*This company's scope of business is very broad.*
- 会计 (kuàijì, accountant/accounting): 她大学学的是会计专业。*She majored in accounting in college.*
- 报告 (bàogào, report): 请把报告发到我的邮箱。*Please send the report to my email.*

### Media, public discourse and debate

- 媒体 (méitǐ, media): 这条新闻被很多媒体报道了。*This news was reported by many media outlets.*
- 宣传 (xuānchuán, to promote/publicize): 公司花了不少钱做宣传。*The company spent a lot of money on promotion.*
- 辩论 (biànlùn, debate): 明天班里要举行一场辩论赛。*Tomorrow the class is holding a debate.*
- 赞成 (zànchéng, to approve/be in favor of): 大部分人都赞成这个方案。*Most people are in favor of this plan.*
- 反对 (fǎnduì, to oppose): 他反对这个决定。*He opposes this decision.*
- 否认 (fǒurèn, to deny): 他否认自己犯了错误。*He denies making a mistake.*

### Emotional and psychological states

- 乐观 (lèguān, optimistic): 不管发生什么,她总是很乐观。*No matter what happens, she's always optimistic.*
- 悲观 (bēiguān, pessimistic): 别这么悲观,事情还有希望。*Don't be so pessimistic, there's still hope.*
- 灰心 (huīxīn, discouraged): 失败了一次,不要灰心。*You failed once — don't get discouraged.*
- 勇气 (yǒngqì, courage): 她鼓起勇气说出了真相。*She summoned the courage to tell the truth.*
- 忍不住 (rěnbuzhù, can't help but): 他忍不住笑了出来。*He couldn't help but laugh.*

### Society, economy and gradual change

- 竞争 (jìngzhēng, competition/to compete): 现在的就业竞争越来越激烈。*Job competition is getting more and more intense.*
- 逐渐 (zhújiàn, gradually): 天气逐渐变冷了。*The weather is gradually getting colder.*
- 尽管 (jǐnguǎn, although): 尽管很累,他还是坚持完成了工作。*Although he was exhausted, he still insisted on finishing the work.*
- 究竟 (jiūjìng, exactly/after all): 你究竟想说什么?*What exactly are you trying to say?*
- 毕竟 (bìjìng, after all): 毕竟他还是个孩子,你别太严格。*He's still a child after all — don't be too strict.*

## Study plan

- 1,300 new words over roughly 10 weeks = ~19 new cards/day
- Prioritize the abstract/opinion cluster first — it shows up in every HSK 5 reading passage and in the writing section
- Every card gets one example sentence. Abstract words with no sentence are nearly impossible to recall later, because there's no image to hang the meaning on
- Review new HSK 5 cards alongside due HSK 3–4 reviews so the vocabulary doesn't sit in isolation

At this volume, the scheduling algorithm matters more than it did at HSK 1–2. Fixed intervals (Anki's old SM-2 default) waste reviews on words you already know and under-review the ones you keep forgetting. FSRS instead tracks each card's individual difficulty and predicted memory decay, spacing reviews per-card instead of per-deck — see [FSRS vs. SM-2: what changed](/tips/fsrs-vs-sm2-what-changed) for the mechanics. On a deck this size, that's the gap between finishing in ten weeks and giving up in week four.

## Why examples matter even more at HSK 5

At HSK 2, a bare translation was usually enough — 快 just means fast. At HSK 5, a bare translation actively misleads you. 究竟 and 毕竟 both loosely translate as "after all," but they behave completely differently in a sentence (one asks, one concludes). 观念 and 理论 both gloss as something like "idea," but you'd never swap them. The only way these distinctions stick is by reading the word inside a real sentence, every time you review it — which is also why radical-level decomposition, covered in [how to memorize Chinese characters faster](/tips/memorize-chinese-characters-faster-srs), stops being enough on its own at this stage. You need the character, the sentence, and the contrast with its near-synonyms.
